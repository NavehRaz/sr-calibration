\section{What survival data identify}\label{sec:identifiability}

A survival curve is the distribution of one number per individual, the first-passage time of $X$ to the threshold.
Not every parameter of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sr} can be recovered from it. This section states which combinations can, which
cannot, and the rules for reporting them that follow; Section~\ref{sec:val-ident} gives the evidence.
Table~\ref{tab:identified} lists every quantity a user might report, says whether survival data pin it and how to
report it, and gives the coverage and error that the released calibrator achieved on the benchmark.

\paragraph{The threshold cancels.} The rescaling $X\to X/\xc$ of Section~\ref{sec:model} leaves the first-passage
distribution unchanged, so $\xc$ is not a parameter of the survival problem. It is fixed at $\xc=1$ at no cost, and
every damage-valued parameter ($\beta$, $\kappa$, and through $\eps$ the noise) is read in units of the threshold.
After this reduction four parameters remain, and they are still not all identified.

\paragraph{What a survival curve pins.} Production ($\eta$) and noise ($\eps$) are identified, as is the
time-scale-free combination $\sqrt\eta/\eps$ (it is unchanged by time rescaling, $(\eta,\eps)\to(a^2\eta,a\eps)$).
Removal enters only through the saturating term $X/(X+\kappa)$, and a survival curve constrains the removal rate
\emph{averaged over the damage range that trajectories traverse}, $\beta\,\rbar(\kappa)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:rbar}
\citep{raz2026damage}, not its value at any one damage level. This mean removal is a closed interval for \C{ident-rbar-closed-panel}
of \C{ident-panel-valid} valid cohorts of the calibration panel and \C{ident-rbar-closed-medium} of \C{ident-medium-valid} of the
medium tier, where the closed intervals are about \C{ident-rbar-width-lo} to \C{ident-rbar-width-hi} decades wide; on the
others it is an upper bound, which is still an answer (a ``removal at most this large''). What the curve does not
pin is $\kappa$, the removal at the threshold $\beta/(1+\kappa)$ (equivalently the time $\beta/(\eta(1+\kappa))$ at which
removal at the threshold balances production), and $\xc$. In particular it does not show whether removal saturates
($\kappa\lesssim1$) or is nearly linear ($\kappa\gg1$); the evidence is at the moderate cohort sizes of the
calibration panel, and whether much larger cohorts can see saturation has not been tested.

\paragraph{The ridge.} The profile likelihood is nearly flat along the curve
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ridge}
  \beta(\kappa)\,\rbar(\kappa) = c, \qquad \text{i.e.}\quad \beta(\kappa)=\frac{c}{\rbar(\kappa)},
\end{equation}
on which $\eta$, $\eps$ and the mean removal $c$ are fixed while $\kappa$ moves. Because $\rbar(\kappa)\to1$ for
$\kappa\to0$ and $\rbar(\kappa)\simeq 1/(2\kappa)$ for $\kappa\gg1$, raw $\beta$ moves by a factor of about $2\kappa$
along the ridge, and the removal at the threshold, $c/[\rbar(\kappa)(1+\kappa)]$, by a factor that approaches $2$
(\C{ident-log2} decades). Across the pinned values of $\kappa$ from $10^{-3}$ to $300$, the log-likelihood of every
valid panel cohort but one stayed within the one-parameter $95\,\%$ threshold of $1.92$ nats of its best value
(\C{ridge-valley-n} of \C{ridge-valley-of}; the exception, a stale optimiser stop, is discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:val-ident}), and the median drift across these slices was \C{ridge-drift-eta} decades for $\eta$,
\C{ridge-drift-rbar} for $\beta\rbar$, \C{ridge-drift-kp1} for $\beta/(1+\kappa)$ and \C{ridge-drift-beta} for raw $\beta$.

\paragraph{Pinning $\kappa$ is nearly lossless if removal is read as $\beta\rbar$.} Fixing $\kappa=\kappa_0$ with
$\kappa_0=10^{-3}$ gives $\rbar(\kappa_0)=\C{ident-rbar-k0}$, so the fitted $\beta$ is in effect the mean removal. On
\C{kfix-J-n} cohorts of an earlier benchmark library (v5), this variant (kfix) lost calibration on a net of at most
\C{kfix-J0001-net-max} of them for any identified quantity, used
\C{kfix-J0001-cpu} of the CPU time of the free fit, and reports $\beta$ as ``$\beta$ at $\kappa_0$'' and never $\kappa$
(Section~\ref{sec:val-ident}). Pinning is biased when removal is read at the threshold, because that quantity moves along the
ridge, in the direction of the pinned $\kappa_0$.

\paragraph{Reporting rules.} These follow from the above and apply to every calibrator variant.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item Report $\eta$ and $\eps$ as values (closed intervals), and as bounds where an end is open; $\sqrt\eta/\eps$ likewise.
  \item Report removal as the mean removal $\beta\rbar(\kappa)$ or the ratio $\beta\rbar(\kappa)/\eta$, as a value or a bound
    according to its interval. Raw $\beta$ is a bound, or ``$\beta$ at $\kappa_0$'' under a pinned variant.
  \item Never report $\kappa$ or the removal at the threshold $\beta/(1+\kappa)$. The calibrator declines $\kappa$ on
    \C{ident-kappa-declined-pct}\,\% of the \C{gate-v6-n} reference cohorts (Section~\ref{sec:val-ident}).
  \item One-sided bounds are not $95\,\%$ bounds. Pooled over the reference cohorts of all arms, the one-sided
    bounds on $\kappa$ and on $\beta$ covered the truth in \C{ident-onesided-kappa-pct}\,\% and \C{ident-onesided-beta-pct}\,\%
    of cases (Section~\ref{sec:val-benchmark}); state them as ``at least'' or ``at most'' and give no confidence level.
  \item The derived ballistic score $B=\beta\rbar(\kappa)/(\eta L)$ of Section~\ref{sec:model} is a function of $\eta$ and the mean
    removal. It is as well determined as $\beta\rbar$ and no better: where $\beta\rbar$ is only an upper bound, so is $B$, and it is
    never quoted with more precision than the interval of $\beta\rbar/\eta$.
\end{enumerate}
