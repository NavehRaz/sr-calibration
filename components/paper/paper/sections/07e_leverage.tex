\subsection{Leverage check}\label{sec:val-leverage}

\paragraph{The rule.} After each fit the calibrator ranks the individuals by the first-order shift that dropping them would
cause in $\log\eta$, $\log\eps$, $\log(\eta/\eps)$ and $\log(\beta\rbar/\eta)$, refits the cohort without the top-ranked individual
(twice: the calibrator's own first fitting stage, and the optimiser started at the reported estimate), and compares the
refit with the reported estimate on the reduced cohort's own likelihood. The cohort is flagged when one of the four quantities
moves by more than $1.5\times$ \emph{and} the refit gains more than $1.92$ nats, the one-parameter $95\,\%$ threshold. A
larger move at a smaller gain is reported as a slide along a flat likelihood ridge, not a flag. The check never changes the
estimate or the intervals. % src: sraging docs/algorithms/sections/leverage.tex; leverage_rule.py

\paragraph{Nulls.} If no individual is influential, the check should not flag. It flagged none of \C{lev-null-heldout-n}
held-out cohorts of the benchmark library ($n=\C{lev-heldout-size}$), none of \C{lev-null-smalln-n} small cohorts ($n=\C{lev-smalln-size}$),
and none of \C{lev-null-twins-n} twin cohorts, \C{lev-null-heldout-flags}, \C{lev-null-smalln-flags} and
\C{lev-null-twins-flags} flags; the exact binomial one-sided $95\,\%$ upper bounds on the false-flag rate are
\C{lev-null-heldout-ub}\,\%, \C{lev-null-smalln-ub}\,\% and \C{lev-null-twins-ub}\,\%. A pilot panel of \C{lev-p3-n} cohorts of \C{lev-p3-nmin} to
\C{lev-p3-nmax} individuals also gave \C{lev-p3-flags} false flags. The size of the null is what limits these bounds: with
\C{lev-null-heldout-n} cohorts the rate cannot be shown to be below about a fifth. The second criterion is
needed. Dropping one individual moved one of the four quantities by more than $1.5\times$ on \C{lev-null-slide-heldout} of the
\C{lev-null-heldout-n} held-out cohorts and on \C{lev-null-slide-smalln} of the \C{lev-null-smalln-n} small cohorts, with a likelihood gain below $1.92$ nats: a move along a flat ridge, not influence. In the pilot panel these slides were all
moves of the removal combination $\beta\rbar/\eta$, which is where the ridge acts.% src: leverage.tex, evidence

\paragraph{Planted individuals.} To measure power, a single death was added to simulated cohorts. A death at age zero, which
the model assigns negligible probability, was flagged in \C{lev-age0-detected-n100} of \C{lev-age0-reps} replicates at
$n=100$, \C{lev-age0-detected-n300} of \C{lev-age0-reps} at $n=300$ and \C{lev-age0-detected-n1000} of \C{lev-age0-reps} at $n=1000$.
A planted death late in life, which moves the fit less, was flagged in \C{lev-late-detected-n100}, \C{lev-late-detected-n300} and
\C{lev-late-detected-n1000} of \C{lev-age0-reps} at the same sizes, so the power for a late individual is not monotone in $n$ in
this small experiment.

\paragraph{Overhead.} On the pilot panel the check added a median of \C{lev-overhead-median}\,\% to the fit's CPU time
(ninetieth percentile \C{lev-overhead-p90}\,\%).

\paragraph{Limits.} The check runs in the released calibrator both without (J) and with an external hazard (H), but its error rates were measured
for J only, on cohorts simulated from the model, and not separately on cohorts in which removal is strongly identified. (i) It drops one individual, so a cluster of similar early deaths acting together is not seen:
removing one of them moves nothing while the others remain. (ii) Its error rates are measured on a few dozen cohorts at the sizes above;
the ratio $1.5$ is a convention that was measured, not calibrated. (iii) On very large human cohorts with parameters at the
edge of the search box the refit cannot move from the reported estimate, and the check returns ``not assessed''.
(iv) The convergence of the refit could be checked for only \C{lev-smalln-measured} of the \C{lev-null-smalln-n} small null cohorts,
because the stored acceptance records of the rest do not hold the estimate. % src: sraging tests/acceptance/results/lev_small_n_003g.json
It checks fits of single cohorts; the effect fits of Section~\ref{sec:effects} (a multiplier within a pair of groups) have no drop-one check and are outside its evidence.
