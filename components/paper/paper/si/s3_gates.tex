The released calibrator (version 1.0.1) passed two release gates before it became the default, both specified before the
records were read. Table~\ref{tab:gates} summarises them. They are acceptance tests of a software release, not
measurements of statistical performance; the performance results are in Sections~\ref{sec:val-benchmark}--\ref{sec:val-leverage}.

\paragraph{GATE-V6.} Run on the \C{gate-v6-n} reference cohorts of library v6 (main and big-$n$ groups) with the released
calibrator, the previous calibrator (v0.3.3) and an uninformative-prior null, in five gates. G1 compares calibration
against v0.3.3, per arm and quantity, with a paired two-sided and a one-sided test. G2 uses a truth witness: on
informative cohorts, the log-likelihood at the truth minus that at the fitted estimate must not exceed $1.92$ nats more
often than for v0.3.3. G3 asks that availability and floored accuracy do not lose on the pure arm. G4 is a registry of
\C{gate-v6-registry-total} automatic checks of the benchmark harness itself: \C{gate-v6-registry-passed} passed, \C{gate-v6-registry-warned}
warned and \C{gate-v6-registry-failed} failed. The warnings are not failures of the calibrator. Four checks cannot be run against
a record that does not carry the profile or the likelihood at the truth, three do not apply to this call and one is not wired; of the others, one flags that
\C{gate-v6-g14-low} of \C{gate-v6-g14-n} values of $\kappa$ in the library are represented by fewer than twenty distinct manifold points, and one that
\C{gate-v6-g2cells-low} of \C{gate-v6-g2cells-n} strata has fewer than ten cells. G5 re-evaluates the stored estimates and checks
the integrity of each record.

\paragraph{The default-switch gate.} The gate of the specification that made the released calibrator the default has five
pre-registered rules per stratum: PR-1, the truth witness (no valid cohort with $W>1.92$); PR-2, coverage inside the
reference set against v0.3.3; PR-3, coverage outside the reference set, which has no v0.3.3 comparator, against the released
calibrator's own coverage on the same arm and quantity inside it (not lower by more than $0.10$ at one-sided $95\,\%$); PR-4,
intervals narrower than the null's; PR-5, the predecessor shown failing where a defect was being fixed. The stored file
holds \C{gate-003g-n-verdicts} verdicts and its status is ``blocked; decided (\C{gate-003g-decided} of \C{gate-003g-blocking})'': every blocking entry was
referred to the project lead, who accepted each as a stated limit. They are of three kinds. Three strata outside the reference set have only
a handful of closed claims (one to three), so PR-3 cannot pass at that sample size whatever the coverage; every closed claim there covered the
truth. For $\beta$ in two strata there were too few closed claims to test at all. And in one stratum the failure is a low count that is
compatible with equal coverage: for $\eta$ on censored cohorts, \C{gate-003g-censor-hits} of \C{gate-003g-censor-n} intervals covered the truth
against \C{gate-003g-censor-in-hits} of \C{gate-003g-censor-in-n} inside the reference set (lower bound of the difference
\C{gate-003g-censor-lb}), a count that has probability \C{gate-003g-censor-p} under exactly equal coverage. One cohort of one stratum is not stored
(its job was preempted repeatedly), which is the PR-1 entry. The file also records \C{gate-003g-deviations} deviations from the rules, each with the
decision that accepted it.
